Exam 1

  1. Question

    According to the Lewis definition of acid-base reactivity, which of the following statements correctly defines a Lewis acid?


    1. A chemical species that accepts an electron pair to form a new coordinate covalent bond using a vacant low-energy orbital.
    2. A chemical species that donates a proton (H+\text{H}^+) via heterolytic cleavage of a polarized X−H\text{X}-\text{H} bond.
    3. Any chemical species bearing a net positive formal charge, such as the tetramethylammonium cation ((CH3)4N+(\text{CH}_3)_4\text{N}^+), regardless of valence octet occupancy.
    4. A chemical species that increases the concentration of hydroxide ions (OH−\text{OH}^-) when dissolved in an aqueous solvent medium.
    5. A chemical species that donates an unshared electron pair from a filled nonbonding orbital to form a coordinate covalent bond.

    Solution

    Under the Lewis acid-base framework (McMurry Organic Chemistry, 10th ed., Section 2.11: Acids and Bases: The Lewis Definition), acid-base reactions are defined entirely by the transfer and sharing of electron pairs rather than proton transfer:

    1. Lewis Acid Definition and Orbital Requirements: A Lewis acid is an electron-pair acceptor (electrophile). To accept an incoming pair of nonbonding electrons and form a new coordinate covalent (dative) bond, the Lewis acid must possess a vacant low-energy orbital (such as an empty unhybridized 2p2p orbital, an accessible low-energy atomic orbital, or an unoccupied low-energy molecular orbital / LUMO).
      • Representative Non-Protic Lewis Acids: Boron trifluoride (BF3\text{BF}_3, molar mass 67.805g/mol67.805\ \text{g/mol}) and aluminum trichloride (AlCl3\text{AlCl}_3, molar mass 133.332g/mol133.332\ \text{g/mol}) contain group 13 central atoms with only six valence electrons (sp2sp^2-hybridized) and an open, vacant pp orbital.
      • Protic Lewis Acids: A bare proton (H+\text{H}^+, atomic mass 1.008g/mol1.008\ \text{g/mol}) acts as a Lewis acid because it possesses an empty 1s1s orbital capable of accommodating two electrons.
    2. Lewis Bases and Coordinate Covalent Bond Formation: A Lewis base is an electron-pair donor (nucleophile) that possesses a filled nonbonding orbital (lone pair) or electron-rich π\pi bond. In a Lewis acid-base reaction, curved arrows track the heterolytic movement of two electrons from the Lewis base lone pair to the vacant orbital of the Lewis acid: BF3+(CH3)2O⇌F3B−–O+(CH3)2\text{BF}_3 + (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{F}_3\text{B}^{-}\,\text{--}\,\text{O}^{+}(\text{CH}_3)_2
      • Reactant Lewis base: Dimethyl ether ((CH3)2O(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{O}, molar mass 46.069g/mol46.069\ \text{g/mol}) donates an sp3sp^3 oxygen lone pair.
      • Adduct product: The boron-ether coordinate complex (molar mass 113.874g/mol113.874\ \text{g/mol}) forms with a formal charge shift (ΔFC=+1\Delta FC = +1 on oxygen, ΔFC=−1\Delta FC = -1 on boron). During adduct formation, boron reorganizes from trigonal planar (sp2sp^2) to tetrahedral (sp3sp^3) geometry.
    3. Analysis of Misconceptions and Fallacies:
      • Proton Transfer: Donating H+\text{H}^+ defines a Brønsted-Lowry acid, which is a specialized subset of acid-base behavior. Species without any hydrogen atoms (such as BF3\text{BF}_3, AlCl3\text{AlCl}_3, FeCl3\text{FeCl}_3, and TiCl4\text{TiCl}_4) cannot donate protons but are powerful Lewis acids.
      • Charge vs. Octet Availability: A positive formal charge does not automatically confer Lewis acidity. The tetramethylammonium ion ((CH3)4N+(\text{CH}_3)_4\text{N}^+, molar mass 74.147g/mol74.147\ \text{g/mol}) bears a positive charge but has four completed C−N\text{C}-\text{N} covalent bonds (an eight-electron octet) and no low-energy vacant orbitals; nitrogen cannot expand its valence shell, so (CH3)4N+(\text{CH}_3)_4\text{N}^+ cannot function as a Lewis acid.

    1. Correct. A Lewis acid is defined as an electron-pair acceptor that accommodates an electron pair from a Lewis base into a vacant, low-energy orbital to form a coordinate covalent bond.
    2. Incorrect. Donating a proton (H+\text{H}^+) defines a Brønsted-Lowry acid. While an ejected H+\text{H}^+ is itself a Lewis acid, the parent molecule donating it is functioning as a Brønsted acid, and non-protic species like BF3\text{BF}_3 act as Lewis acids without possessing or donating protons.
    3. Incorrect. Possessing a positive charge does not ensure Lewis acidity. Cations with complete valence octets and no low-energy vacant orbitals, such as (CH3)4N+(\text{CH}_3)_4\text{N}^+, cannot accept an electron pair without violating the octet rule.
    4. Incorrect. Increasing aqueous hydroxide ion (OH−\text{OH}^-) concentration defines an Arrhenius base. The Lewis acid-base definition is broader, solvent-independent, and focused on electron-pair sharing.
    5. Incorrect. Donating an unshared electron pair defines a Lewis base (nucleophile), not a Lewis acid. Lewis acids are electron-pair acceptors (electrophiles).
